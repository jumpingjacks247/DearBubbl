- I’m entering the Presidential SI Challenge (Standard Track), a national K–12 competition. My age group: Middle school (grades 6–8)
- Judges score only a written entry (a PDF of up to 10 pages) and a video of up to 4 minutes. They reward a clear understanding of the problem and a sensible use of AI.
- My deadlines: You can't miss the registration deadline and the entry deadline in late December.
## The problem
- People in school, teen ages/puberty struggle with expressing their emotions because they feel unsafe sharing their ideas, opnions and feelings because they are afraid others will judge them.
- Impact area: Safety and kindness
- Why it matters (impact): My project will help them better express their feelings, sharing their ideas and opinions and learning how to improve from depression, social anxiety etc. Depression and social anxiety never really goes away, but it always heals.
- Evidence it’s real: 54% uncomfortable talking to teachers, meaning they struggle opening up to superior adults. https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2025/04/22/teens-social-media-and-mental-health/?utm_source=chatgpt.com
- What people told me: QUESTIONS:
#1: "Who do you confide in with your troubles, pressure etc.?"
#2: "Why don't you try and talk to others? Peers, not teachers."

TRAITS:
11-year-old girl for Person A
13-year-old boy for Person B

Person A: 
Response to #1: "Sometimes I talk to my mom because I know she won't judge me, but I don't really tell my friends everything. I'm scared they might think I'm weird or tell other people."
Response to #2: "Because I'm scared they'll judge me or think I'm being dramatic. Sometimes when I share something personal, people make jokes about it or tell their other friends."

## My solution
- My AI’s job: My AI helps by talking to the student, encouraging them to open up to peers. Encouraging them to speak up for their opinions, which works better than teachers, parents, and therapists trying to push them out of their comfort zone to talk because that puts a lot of pressure and likely makes the problem worse than it already is.
- Tools that already exist, and how mine is different: Soluna offers many mental health tools and many online, real therapists. However, mine focuses on taking one step at a time in their comfort zone, to slowly step out of it and stretch their confidence. That includes talking to others more, and being able to interact, and express feelings and opnions confidently.
- Safety plan: #1: The AI is encouraging the user to harm the person that hurt them physically. I will give the AI a specific rule about not encouraging physical harm. 
#2: The user sharing too much private information. (Address, etc.) The chat will remind the user to not enter private information before, and during the chat to insure user comfort and safety. 
#3: The user becoming too dependent on the AI. I will be making sure that the AI gives constant reminders of daily goals, for example, waving hi to someone they've never met, or sit with someone to talk. 
#4: The AI should never be allowed to give judgemental, mean, rude comments about the user's feelings. 
#5: If the user shares something private, like abuse, threatening in the family, etc. The AI will show a helpline, for example 911, or 988. 
*I will make sure that the student isn't pressured to share anything with the AI without their consent, and the user has any and every right to leave the app whenever they please.*
## My AI’s instructions
- When it answers: The AI prompts the user when they first enter: "Hello! I'm Bubbl, here to help you and those around you with expressing feelings and social anxiety. What are we talking about today? :)" Then there's 3 options: "Talk about your day?" "What happened at home?" "Everything okay? :(" The user answers with clicking an option, then talking about the option reply, then they press "Send."
- Its job: The AI is supposed to seem like a comfort character vibe, comfort zone, and a person that actually cares about you. It encourages you without ever pushing you out of your comfort zone without consent. It has a sense of humor, and makes your day better.
- Who it talks to and how to sound: The AI is responding to the user, and it should sound sad and depressing to the AI. The AI's job is to encourage them, brigthen their day, and try and get them to talk to others. This solves the problem about someone becoming to dependent on the AI.
- Facts it must use: Fact: A UNICEF and Johns Hopkins University report found that teens in many countries worried about being judged, mocked, or excluded if they talked about mental health. Many also said they had trouble finding the right words to explain their feelings.
Fact: The CDC found that in 2023, 39.7% of U.S. high school students felt very sad or hopeless for at least two weeks, and 28.5% reported poor mental health.
Fact: Pew Research Center found that 41% of teens feel at least some pressure to fit in socially. This pressure can make people worry about what others think of them.
- Rules it must follow: The AI must *never* be ignorant, dismissive about the user's feelings and opnions. Though the AI doesn't have to agree with everything the user says, it should still be a encouraging, supportive, friendly AI.
- How to answer: The AI should answer sympathetically, and encouragingly. It shouldn't be particularly long, maybe 1 sentence to 2 sentences at a time. It should use some emojis: ":(", ":)",":3" etc. But *never* a overuse of these emojis. Maybe once or twice.
